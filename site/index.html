What is AirWSJT-X?

AirWSJT-X merges AirScout aircraft tracking with WSJT-X weak-signal digital modes to automate aircraft scatter contacts on 1296 MHz (23 cm) and higher microwave bands.

It treats commercial aircraft as dynamic, low-orbit reflectors. When a viable aircraft is detected between two stations, AirWSJT-X locks the rotator, configures WSJT-X for Q65-15s operation, and manages the entire QSO sequence — from airplane selection through contact completion.

The mobile app brings standalone aircraft tracking to the field — a compass rose and elevation arc for hand-pointing antennas at aircraft, with speech callouts for hands-free operation. No WSJT-X or rotator required.

Features

Aircraft Tracking

Real-time airplane position tracking with automatic selection of viable scatter targets between two configured stations.

Rotator Lock

Automated rotator control via Hamlib — locks antenna bearing to the selected aircraft and maintains tracking stability.

WSJT-X Integration

Controls WSJT-X via UDP protocol for Q65-15s mode on 23 cm+ bands. Manages frequency, mode, and QSO sequencing.

Contact State Machine

Full QSO state machine manages the contact from CQ through 73, with automatic retry and timeout handling.

Mobile Compass Rose New

Visual aircraft positions on a compass display with bearing needle and elevation arc — designed for hand-pointing in the field.

Speech Callouts New

Audible azimuth and elevation announcements for the selected aircraft, so you can point without looking at the screen.

Callsign Database New

Offline callsign lookup via callook.info with IndexedDB storage on mobile — identify operators without an internet connection.

Modern Desktop UI

Built with Tauri + React — lightweight native app with station config, plane lock panel, and rotator status display.

How It Works

Desktop (Full Mode)

AirWSJT-X enables Q65-15s operation only when all conditions are met:

  • Two stations are configured (your station + target station)
  • A viable aircraft is detected and selected as a scatter target
  • Rotator lock is acquired and remains stable
  • WSJT-X is configured for a supported 23 cm+ band (1296 MHz and up)
  • The operator explicitly starts the QSO

Mobile (Standalone Tracker)

The mobile app operates independently — no WSJT-X or rotator needed:

  • Enter your grid locator in settings
  • Aircraft in your area appear on the compass rose
  • Tap an aircraft to see its azimuth, elevation, and distance
  • Speech callouts guide antenna pointing hands-free

Architecture

AirWSJT-X is a Tauri desktop application (Rust + React frontend) with a Python backend for radio and tracking logic. The mobile app runs standalone TypeScript — no Python sidecar needed.

FAQ

Is AirWSJT-X safe for airplanes?

Yes — AirWSJT-X has absolutely no effect on aircraft or aviation systems. It is a passive ham radio tool that reads publicly available aircraft position data (the same data used by flight-tracking apps like Flightradar24 and ADS-B Exchange) to identify airplanes flying between two ham radio stations. When a plane is in the right geometric position, operators can bounce weak VHF/UHF radio signals off the aircraft's metal fuselage to communicate with each other — a technique called aircraft scatter.

  • No equipment is installed on the airplane. The aircraft is simply a passive reflector, like a building or a mountain.
  • No frequencies are shared with aviation. Ham radio bands are completely separate from aircraft communication and navigation frequencies.
  • No signals are transmitted to the airplane. The radio energy that reaches the fuselage is extremely weak (far less than a typical WiFi router) and reflects naturally off the metal surface.
  • No aircraft systems are affected. The crew, passengers, avionics, and navigation are completely unaware of and unaffected by the process.

Aircraft scatter is a well-established technique in amateur radio that has been used safely for decades.

What's the difference between desktop and mobile?

The desktop app is the full aircraft scatter automation system — it connects to WSJT-X, controls your rotator, and manages the complete QSO workflow. The mobile app is a standalone aircraft tracker designed for field use — it shows you where aircraft are relative to your position, with a compass display and speech callouts for hand-pointing antennas.
